## Introduction
The paper presents a systematic literature review analyzing the multi-tiered landscape of enterprise AI platform taxonomy, adoption dynamics, and cybersecurity risk classification. The review introduces five author-derived synthesis instruments: the [[Composite Adoption Score (CAS)]], the [[Enterprise AI Risk Classification Framework (EARCF)]], the [[Enterprise AI Platform Taxonomy (EAPT)]], the [[Enterprise AI Governance Maturity Model (EAGM)]], and the [[Enterprise AI Governance Lifecycle (EAGL)]].

## Methodology
The review evaluates an extensive body of retrieved records under a rigorous, multi-framework protocol compliant with [[PRISMA]], [[Kitchenham]], and [[Webster Watson]] standards. High-quality primary sources spanning peer-reviewed literature, international standards, and authoritative industry disclosures were synthesized.

## Findings
The review finds that generative AI adoption expanded rapidly across commercial segments, driven primarily by Tier-1 productivity suites and enterprise infrastructure APIs. The emergence of agentic architectures characterized by multi-step reasoning and autonomous tool-use capabilities has introduced acute vulnerabilities, including:

* [[Prompt Injection]]
* [[Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) corpus poisoning]]
* Excessive agency

Threat modeling demonstrates that while traditional security controls mitigate baseline boundary failures, they are less effective against autonomous state manipulation and memory-auditing lapses.

## Governance Maturity Roadmap
The paper concludes by presenting a governance maturity roadmap designed to support the transition from ad hoc mitigation practices to auditable, zero-trust AI architectures. The roadmap identifies significant gaps in multi-agent accountability protocols and memory-state auditing, including:

* [[NIST AI RMF]]
* [[ISO/IEC 42001]]
* [[OWASP LLM Top 10]]
* [[MITRE ATLAS]]

## Limitations
The authors acknowledge the limitations of their study, including the identified gaps in multi-agent accountability protocols and memory-state auditing.

## Conclusion
The paper provides a systematic review of the security risks and governance of enterprise generative AI. The findings highlight the need for a governance maturity roadmap to support the transition from ad hoc mitigation practices to auditable, zero-trust AI architectures.
