# Transforming Software Development with Generative AI: Empirical Insights on Collaboration and Workflow

## Abstract Summary

[[Generative AI]] (GenAI) is fundamentally changing how [[knowledge workers]], specifically [[software developers]], approach tasks and [[collaboration]]. Tools like [[ChatGPT]] and [[GitHub Copilot]] offer new opportunities to assist and augment developers. An [[empirical study]] involving [[interviews]] with 13 diverse professionals (data scientists, managers, developers, designers, frontend developers) revealed that [[ChatGPT]] represents a [[paradigm shift]] in software development workflows. The technology empowers developers by enhancing [[efficiency]], accelerating the [[learning process]], and increasing [[motivation]] through the reduction of [[tedious and repetitive tasks]]. However, the study also indicates a shift in [[teamwork collaboration]], where engineers rely on [[GenAI]] for assistance instead of [[co-workers]], which negatively impacts the [[learning loop]] in [[agile teams]].

## Research Questions / Claims / Hypotheses

*   **Primary Research Question:** How are [[software engineer]] [[work practices]] transformed by [[Generative AI]], and what is the potential impact of this transformation on [[collaboration]]?
*   **Claim 1:** [[ChatGPT]] signifies a [[paradigm shift]] in the workflow of [[software developers]].
*   **Claim 2:** [[Generative AI]] empowers [[software developers]] by enabling them to work more [[efficiently]], speed up the [[learning process]], and increase [[motivation]] by reducing [[tedious and repetitive tasks]].
*   **Claim 3:** [[Generative AI]] introduces a change in [[teamwork collaboration]] due to [[software engineers]] using [[GenAI]] for help instead of asking [[co-workers]], which impacts the [[learning loop]] in [[agile teams]].
*   **Implicit Hypothesis:** The introduction of [[Generative AI]] is likely to affect core [[teamwork components]] as defined by the [[Dickinson and McIntyre]] model and later adapted by [[Moe et al.]] for [[agile teamwork]].

## Methodologies, Algorithms, and System Architecture

*   **Study Design:** Exploratory multi-case study [[Yin_2009]].
*   **Context:** Conducted within two research programs focused on [[software development processes]] where companies had introduced [[Generative AI]] into their product development.
*   **Sampling Method:** [[Snowball sampling]] [[Biernacki_1981]] was used to recruit informants through Slack, targeting subjects with diverse [[GenAI]] usage.
*   **Data Collection Method:** Semi-structured [[interviews]].
    *   An interview guide was developed based on a literature review.
    *   Example questions included: "How do you use GenAI services?" and "Which effects do you get from using them?".
    *   Interviews were conducted by four of the five authors (first, third, fourth, and fifth) to spread out potential subjective biases.
*   **Data Analysis Method:** Two cycles of coding [[Saldana_2015]].
    *   **Cycle 1 (Initial 8 interviews):** Descriptive and initial coding [[Miles_and_Huberman_1994]] performed by the first and third authors. A discussion between these authors led to emerging categories and themes, and a revised interview guide.
    *   **Cycle 2 (Remaining 5 interviews & full dataset):** The fourth author performed descriptive coding on the new interviews. The first author performed focused coding on all interviews.
    *   Observed themes and categories were merged through mutual workshops and discussions involving all authors.
*   **Tools under investigation (as reported by informants):**
    *   [[ChatGPT]] (most common for code and text)
    *   [[GitHub Copilot]] (most common for code and text)
    *   [[Midjourney]] (for image creation)
    *   [[DALL-E 2]] (for image creation)
