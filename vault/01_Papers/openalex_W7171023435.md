# REIMAGINING INTELLIGENT INFORMATION SYSTEMS: EMERGING AI PARADIGMS IN LIBRARY AND INFORMATION SCIENCE

**Authors**: Dr. David A. AGBO,, Onyinye R. Ekere, Dr. Ugonna B FIDELUGWUOWO
**Published**: 2026-04-12 | **Citations**: 0 | **Source**: OpenAlex
**URL**: https://openalex.org/W7171023435

## Executive Summary & Abstract
This study reimagines intelligent information systems by examining the evolution and attention is given to human-centred and augmented intelligence, ethical and value-aligned AI, and adaptive, self-learning systems as critical directions shaping the future of intelligent information environments. Methodologically, the study adopts a qualitative, theory-driven convergence of emerging artificial intelligence (AI) paradigms within LIS. It traces the transition from symbolic, rule-based systems to connectionist, cognitive, and generative models, highlighting how these paradigms collectively redefine intelligence as a multidimensional construct encompassing learning, reasoning, and creativity. The paper situates these developments within LIS, emphasizing their transformative implications for knowledge organization, information retrieval, scholarly communication, and user behaviour. Particular design that integrates conceptual analysis, theory synthesis, and an integrative literature review. Conceptual analysis is used to clarify core constructs, while theory synthesis brings together Symbolic Cognition Theory, Neural Network Theory, the Computational Theory of Mind, and Generative Intelligence and Emergent Systems Theory into a unified framework. The integrative literature review systematically examines interdisciplinary contributions from AI, cognitive science, and information science to identify key patterns and emerging trends. The study argues that intelligent information systems are evolving into hybrid, human-centred, and ethically grounded ecosystems that require interdisciplinary collaboration and critical governance. It concludes that LIS must reposition itself as a central actor in designing, mediating, and regulating AI-driven knowledge systems to ensure alignment with human values, scholarly integrity, and equitable access to information. Keywords: Artificial intelligence, intelligent information systems, Library and Information Science, human-centered AI, generative intelligence
