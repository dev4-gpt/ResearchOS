# Generative AI enhances individual creativity but reduces the collective diversity of novel content

## Executive Summary
This study investigates the causal impact of [[Generative Artificial Intelligence]] (specifically [[Large Language Models]]) on the production of creative writing. Through a randomized online experiment ($N = 292$ writers, evaluated by $N = 600$ peer judges), the authors demonstrate a double-edged sword: access to AI-generated ideas boosts individual-level story quality, creativity, and writer enjoyment—particularly for individuals with lower baseline creativity. However, this individual improvement comes at a systemic cost: the resulting collective output is highly converged, showing significantly lower [[Collective Diversity]] and higher semantic similarity compared to purely human-authored content. This dynamic presents a classic [[Social Dilemma]].

---

## Core Hypotheses & Theoretical Framework

The authors construct a framework to evaluate the tension between **individual enhancement** and **collective homogenization**:

1. **Individual Creativity Enhancement ($H_1$):** Access to LLM-generated ideas provides cognitive scaffolding, reducing the search cost for novel combinations, thereby increasing the evaluated creativity, enjoyment, and writing quality of the final output.
2. **Heterogeneous Treatment Effects ($H_2$):** The benefit of AI scaffolding is inversely proportional to the writer's baseline creative ability. Less creative writers benefit more (catch-up effect) than highly creative writers.
3. **Collective Homogenization ($H_3$):** Because LLMs generate outputs based on centralized probability distributions, human writers anchoring on these ideas produce a corpus with reduced semantic distance, leading to a loss of collective novelty.

## Methodological Design & Experimental Pipeline

```

                              │
                              ▼ (Randomized Allocation)
             ┌────────────────┼────────────────┐
             ▼                ▼                ▼
     [Control Group]   [Treatment 1]    [Treatment 2]
     (No AI assistance)  (1 AI Idea)     (5 AI Ideas)
             │                │                │
             └────────────────┼────────────────┘
                              ▼
                [Short Story Writing Task]
             (Topic: Adventure in jungle/space)
                              │
                              ▼
            [Evaluation Phase (600 Peer Judges)]
           (Likert scale ratings & Embeddings)
```

### 1. Phase 1: Baseline Assessment
Before the writing task, participants completed the **[[Divergent Association Task]] (DAT)** to measure baseline divergent thinking.
* **DAT Task:** Participants input 10 nouns that are as semantically distant from one another as possible.
* **Scoring:** Computed using the mean semantic distance between the words using a pre-trained SpaCy model.

### 2. Phase 2: Experimental Treatment Groups
Writers were randomized into three experimental conditions to write a short story (80–150 words) for a young adult audience:
* **Control Condition ($T_0$):** Human-only. No AI assistance allowed.
* **Treatment 1 ($T_1$):** Provided with **one** 3-sentence story idea generated by GPT-4.
* **Treatment 2 ($T_2$):** Provided with a choice of **five** 3-sentence story ideas generated by GPT-4.

### 3. Phase 3: Evaluation
A separate panel of $N = 600$ evaluators rated the generated stories on a 1–7 Likert scale across three dimensions:
* **Creativity:** Originality and value.
* **Enjoyment:** Reader engagement.
* **Writing Quality:** Style, structure, and grammar.

## Mathematical & Statistical Formulations

### 1. Baseline Divergent Thinking (DAT)
Let $W = \{w_1, w_2, \dots, w_{10}\}$ be the set of 10 words provided by participant $i$. The semantic distance between any pair $(w_j, w_k)$ is:
$$d(w_j, w_k) = 1 - \cos(\mathbf{v}_j, \mathbf{v}_k) = 1 - \frac{\mathbf{v}_j \cdot \mathbf{v}_k}{\|\mathbf{v}_j\| \|\mathbf{v}_k\|}$$
Where $\mathbf{v}$ represents the GloVe embedding vector of the word. The DAT score is the mean distance:
$$\text{DAT}_i = \frac{2}{n(n-1)} \sum_{j < k} d(w_j, w_k) \times 100$$

### 2. Regression Model (Individual Outcomes)
To evaluate the treatment effects and their interaction with baseline creativity ($\text{DAT}_i$), the authors specified the following OLS model:
$$Y_{ij} = \beta_0 + \beta_1 T_{1i} + \beta_2 T_{2i} + \beta_3 \text{DAT}_i + \beta_4 (T_{1i} \times \text{DAT}_i) + \beta_5 (T_{2i} \times \text{DAT}_i) + \mathbf{X}_i \gamma + \epsilon_{ij}$$
* $Y_{ij}$: The rating assigned to story $i$ by evaluator $j$ (Creativity, Enjoyment, Quality).
* $T_{1i}, T_{2i}$: Binary treatment indicators.
* $\text{DAT}_i$: Normalized baseline divergent thinking score of writer $i$.
* $\mathbf{X}_i$: Vector of individual control variables (age, gender, education).

### 3. Collective Diversity (Semantic Similarity)
To measure the collective convergence of stories, the authors calculated the [[Cosine Similarity]] between document-level transformer embeddings:
$$\text{Sim}(S_a, S_b) = \frac{\mathbf{E}(S_a) \cdot \mathbf{E}(S_b)}{\|\mathbf{E}(S_a)\| \|\mathbf{E}(S_b)\|}$$
Where $\mathbf{E}(S)$ is the embedding vector of story $S$ generated by a sentence-transformer model (e.g., `all-MiniLM-L6-v2`).
* Collective Diversity is defined as the inverse of the mean pairwise similarity within a treatment group:
$$\text{Diversity}_T = 1 - \frac{2}{N_T(N_T-1)} \sum_{a < b \in T} \text{Sim}(S_a, S_b)$$

## Quantitative Benchmarks & Key Findings

| Metric / Dimension | Control ($T_0$) | Treatment 1 ($T_1$) | Treatment 2 ($T_2$) | Statistical Significance |
| :--- | :---: | :---: | :---: | :--- |
| **Evaluated Story Creativity** | Baseline | $+5.4\%$ | $+8.1\%$ | $p < 0.01$ |
| **Story Enjoyment** | Baseline | $+2.8\%$ | $+5.1\%$ | $p < 0.05$ |
| **Writing Quality** | Baseline | $+10.1\%$ | $+13.5\%$ | $p < 0.001$ |
| **Within-Group Similarity** | Baseline (0.421) | $+10.7\%$ (0.466) | $+8.9\%$ (0.458) | $p < 0.001$ (Bootstrapped) |

### Moderation by Baseline Creativity (DAT)
* **Low-DAT Writers (Lowest Tercile):** Experienced a **$26.6\%$ boost** in creativity evaluations in $T_1$ and a **$22.8\%$ boost** in $T_2$ relative to the control.
* **High-DAT Writers (Highest Tercile):** Showed **no statistically significant difference** in creativity or quality when using AI ideas compared to writing purely on their own.
* **The "Equalizer" Effect:** Generative AI effectively compressed the quality distribution, elevating poor writers to the baseline of highly creative writers, while failing to extend the ceiling of the highly creative writers.

### The Loss of Collective Diversity
While individuals produced better stories, the structural diversity of the corpus collapsed:
* Stories in $T_1$ (Single Idea) were **$10.7\%$ more semantically similar** to each other than stories in the control group.
* Stories in $T_2$ (Five Ideas) were **$8.9\%$ more semantically similar** to each other than stories in the control group.
* This establishes a causal link between the incorporation of LLM priors and the narrowing of the cultural/creative output space.

## Limitations & Boundary Conditions

1. **Short-Form Writing Constraints:** The study restricted story length to 80–150 words. The dynamics of anchoring and divergence may differ substantially in long-form prose (e.g., novels, screenplays) where structural complexity is higher.
2. **Single-Step AI Scaffolding:** The experiment evaluated a static, single-step prompt intervention (providing ideas before writing). It did not explore iterative co-writing paradigms, where real-time feedback loops between human and machine might yield different divergence dynamics.
3. **Task-Specific Creativity (DAT Generalizability):** While the [[Divergent Association Task]] is a validated measure of verbal divergent thinking, it does not capture domain-specific skills such as emotional depth, narrative pacing, or stylistic prose control.
4. **Homogenous LLM Engine:** All AI prompts were generated by a single LLM model family (GPT-4). Diversity loss might be mitigated by employing heterogeneous ensembles of localized, fine-tuned, or temperature-maximized models.
