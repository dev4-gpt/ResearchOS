# PAARA Connector-DPIA Methodology: A Privacy-Aware Risk Assessment Model for Enterprise GenAI Connectors

**Authors**: Vivek Kumar
**Published**: 2026-7-29 | **Citations**: 0 | **Source**: Crossref
**URL**: https://doi.org/10.2139/ssrn.6806638

## Executive Summary & Abstract
Enterprise generative AI tools operate through connector integrations OAuth scopes, Microsoft Graph permissions, REST API bridges, and retrieval-augmented generation pipelines&amp;nbsp; that link large language models to enterprise data sources including email, document repositories, HR systems, finance platforms, and customer databases. Major data protection authorities have published guidance on AI and privacy obligations, but current guidance provides limited operational treatment of connector-layer retrieval governance as a distinct assessment object. Every existing Data Protection Impact Assessment framework frames its analysis around the AI model or product. The integration layer — the OAuth scope, the permission, the retrieval boundary, the prompt-injection vector, the insider-accessible data surface — is treated as an undifferentiated subset of generic information-security controls.&lt;br&gt;&lt;br&gt;This paper introduces the PAARA Connector-DPIA Methodology: a six-component practitioner model for assessing enterprise AI connectors as privacy-relevant and security-relevant processing enablers. The methodology provides a connector inventory framework, WP29 nine-criteria analysis at the connector layer, an OAuth and Graph permission risk register, a retrieval-boundary and prompt-injection threat model, an insider-risk amplification assessment, and a cross-framework alignment table spanning GDPR Article 35, EU AI Act Article 27, ISO/IEC 42005:2025, NIST AI 600-1, OWASP LLM Top 10, and related standards. A worked example and practitioner checklist are included. The methodology is designed to be executable by DPOs, CISOs, and privacy engineers, and submittable to regulatory bodies as a practitioner framework contribution.
