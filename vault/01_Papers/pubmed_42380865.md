## 1. Executive Summary & Analyst Context

This note profiles the ethical, legal, and operational challenges faced by South African **Research Ethics Committees (RECs)** when evaluating protocols involving **Large Language Models (LLMs)** and Artificial Intelligence (AI).

## 2. Regulatory & Legal Landscape

Any AI or LLM-driven research protocol deployed within South Africa must navigate a complex, overlapping web of local legislative frameworks:

```
                  ┌─────────────────────────────────────────┐
                  │      National Health Act 61 of 2003     │
                  │  (Mandates REC oversight for health)    │
                  └────────────────────┬────────────────────┘
                                       │
                                       ▼
                  ┌─────────────────────────────────────────┐
                  │    Protection of Personal Info Act      │
                  │        (POPIA, Act 4 of 2013)           │
                  └────────────────────┬────────────────────┘
                                       │
                                       ▼
                  ┌─────────────────────────────────────────┐
                  │ National Health Research Ethics Council  │
                  │           (NHREC Guidelines)            │
                  └─────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

### 2.1 The Protection of Personal Information Act ([[POPIA]])
*   **Data Processing Limits:** LLM training and fine-tuning on patient records require strict adherence to Section 11 of POPIA (Consent, Justification, and Objection).
*   **Cross-Border Data Flows (Section 72):** Many commercial LLM APIs (e.g., OpenAI, Anthropic) process data on cloud infrastructure located outside South African borders. RECs must scrutinize whether these transfers meet local data adequacy standards.
*   **Special Personal Information (Section 26):** Health data is classified as "special personal information." Processing requires explicit consent or authorization from the Information Regulator.

### 2.2 The National Health Act (NHA) No. 61 of 2003
*   Section 73 mandates that every health research institution must establish or have access to a registered health research ethics committee to review and monitor protocols.

## 3. Methodological Framework: AI Ethical Risk Evaluation

In the absence of harmonized national AI review guidelines, RECs must evaluate LLM protocols using a multi-dimensional risk framework. The evaluation complexity $E_{c}$ of an AI protocol can be modeled as:

$$E_{c} = \mathbf{f}(D_{p}, A_{b}, T_{e}, C_{i})$$

Where:
*   $D_{p}$ = **Data Privacy & Leakage Risk:** Vulnerability of the LLM to membership inference attacks or training data extraction targeting protected health information (PHI).
*   $A_{b}$ = **Algorithmic Bias:** Lack of local representativeness (e.g., LLMs trained predominantly on Western clinical datasets failing to generalize to diverse South African populations).
*   $T_{e}$ = **Transparency/Explainability Deficit:** The "black box" nature of deep learning architectures making auditing clinical decisions impossible.
*   $C_{i}$ = **Informed Consent Integrity:** The challenge of explaining neural network processing and potential downstream data re-use to research participants in a clear, culturally sensitive manner.

## 4. Key Ethical Challenges & REC Deficits

### 4.1 The Technical Competency Gap
*   **The Issue:** Most South African RECs are staffed by clinical trialists, bioethicists, and community representatives who lack formal training in computer science, machine learning architectures, or statistical data modeling.
*   **Consequence:** Protocols utilizing advanced AI systems are either subjected to unnecessary bureaucratic delays due to over-caution, or approved without rigorous auditing of algorithmic parameters.

### 4.2 Data Sovereignty vs. Global AI Infrastructure
*   LLMs require massive infrastructure. Local South African institutions rarely host LLMs locally ($100\text{B}+$ parameter models).
*   **The Conflict:** Transferring clinical data to global servers violates localized sovereignty principles if the host nation lacks equivalent legal frameworks to [[POPIA]].

### 4.3 Demographic & Linguistic Underrepresentation
*   South Africa has 12 official languages. Commercial LLMs perform poorly or hallucinate excessively when processing low-resource indigenous languages (e.g., isiZulu, isiXhosa, Sesotho).
*   RECs must evaluate whether the use of English-only LLMs in clinical tools exacerbates healthcare disparities.

## 5. Structured Comparison: Traditional vs. AI Ethics Review

| Ethical Dimension | Traditional Clinical Trials | LLM & AI Health Research |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Informed Consent** | Static, study-specific. | Dynamic; complex data-sharing pipelines. |
| **Risk of Harm** | Direct physical side effects. | Algorithmic bias, misdiagnosis, loss of privacy. |
| **Data Lifecycle** | Defined storage & destruction timeline. | Persistent parameter weights; indefinite storage. |
| **Explainability** | Biologically plausible mechanism. | Empirical black-box heuristics; unpredictable hallucinations. |
| **Re-evaluation** | Protocol amendments for drug dosages. | Drift in model weights; continuous integration changes. |

---

## 6. Stated Limitations & Future Directions

*   **Policy Lacuna:** There is a lack of explicit, unified national guidelines from the [[NHREC]] specifically tailored to machine learning and LLM governance in clinical research.
*   **Capacity Constraints:** Resource-constrained institutions in South Africa struggle to hire dedicated technical consultants to assist RECs in auditing AI codebases.
*   **Dynamic Nature of AI:** Traditional REC approval is static, whereas LLMs are subject to "data drift" and API updates that can alter model behavior post-approval.
