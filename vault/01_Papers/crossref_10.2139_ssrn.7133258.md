# [[Agentic AI in the Enterprise: A Task-Delegation Taxonomy for Autonomous Agents, Copilots, and Human Expertise]]

## [[Abstract Summary]]

This article addresses the lack of an evidence-based rule for task delegation in organizations deploying [[Generative AI]]. It integrates a systematic evidence synthesis with conceptual development, combining [[Generative-era Experimental Evidence]] (including two original meta-analytic pools) with a conceptual [[Task-Delegation Taxonomy]]. The findings indicate that [[Copilot-style Assistance]] significantly improves output quality and reduces completion time, though these averages are qualified by boundary conditions. The article proposes a four-mode taxonomy for delegation and six decision dimensions, positioning delegation as an evidence-scaled, task-level, and reversible decision.

## [[Problem Statement]]

Organizations deploying [[Generative AI]] lack an evidence-based rule for deciding when a task should be:
1.  Delegated to an [[Autonomous Agent]].
2.  Supported by an [[AI Copilot]].
3.  Reserved for [[Human Expertise]].

## [[Claims and Hypotheses]]

*   [[Copilot-style Assistance]] generally improves output quality.
*   [[Copilot-style Assistance]] generally reduces task completion time.

## [[Methodology]]

The research methodology integrates:
1.  **Systematic Evidence Synthesis**: A review and synthesis of [[Generative-era Experimental Evidence]].
2.  **Conceptual Development**: Formulation of a [[Task-Delegation Taxonomy]].
3.  **Meta-analysis**: Two original meta-analytic pools were conducted on controlled experiments examining [[Copilot-style Assistance]] effects.
    *   **Data Source**: Five controlled experiments per outcome (k=5).
    *   **Outcomes Analyzed**:
        *   Output quality.
        *   Completion time.
    *   **Statistical Measure**: Pooled Standardized Mean Difference (SMD) with 95% Confidence Intervals (CI).

## [[Experimental Results and Benchmarks]]

Across five controlled experiments per outcome, [[Copilot-style Assistance]] demonstrated the following effects:

*   **Output Quality**:
    *   Improved significantly.
    *   Pooled Standardized Mean Difference (SMD): **+0.35**
    *   95% Confidence Interval (CI): **[0.24, 0.47]**
    *   Number of experiments (k): **5**
    *   *Interpretation*: A statistically significant moderate positive effect on output quality.

*   **Completion Time**:
    *   Reduced significantly.
    *   Pooled Standardized Mean Difference (SMD): **-0.66**
    *   95% Confidence Interval (CI): **[-0.80, -0.53]**
    *   Number of experiments (k): **5**
    *   *Interpretation*: A statistically significant large negative effect (reduction) on completion time.

## [[Proposed Framework/Architecture]]

The article proposes a [[Task-Delegation Taxonomy]] integrated with the [[Levels-of-Automation Tradition]].

### [[Four-Mode Taxonomy for Task Delegation]]

1.  [[Human-Only Work]]
2.  [[Copilot Augmentation]]
3.  [[AI Teammates]]
4.  [[Autonomous Agent Delegation]]

### [[Six Decision Dimensions for Task Delegation]]

1.  [[Frontier Position]]
2.  [[Output Verifiability]]
3.  [[Worker Expertise]]
4.  [[Stakes and Reversibility]]
5.  [[Process Observability]]
6.  [[Diversity Externalities]]

### [[Governance Criteria]]

*   The framework includes [[Governance Criteria]] that scale with the degree of autonomy.

### [[Delegation Decision Characterization]]

The framework positions delegation as a decision that is:
*   [[Task-Level]]
*   [[Evidence-Scaled]]
*   [[Reversible Decision]] (rather than a one-time organizational commitment to a single mode of automation).

## [[Limitations]]

The paper acknowledges the following boundary evidence that qualifies the observed average gains from [[Copilot-style Assistance]]:

*   **Experienced Practitioners**: Can experience a slowdown under assistance.
*   **Capability Frontier**: Gains may reverse on tasks that are beyond the current [[AI Capability Frontier]].
*   **Homogenization of Output**: Individual improvements can lead to a homogenization of collective output.
