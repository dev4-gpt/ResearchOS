# Agentic AI in the Enterprise: A Task-Delegation Taxonomy for Autonomous Agents, Copilots, and Human Expertise

**Authors**: Aeron Zentner
**Published**: 2026-7-31 | **Citations**: 0 | **Source**: Crossref
**URL**: https://doi.org/10.2139/ssrn.7133258

## Executive Summary & Abstract
Organizations deploying generative artificial intelligence (AI) lack an evidence-based rule for deciding when a task should be delegated to an autonomous agent, supported by an AI copilot, or reserved for human expertise. Integrating a systematic evidence synthesis with conceptual development, this article addresses that gap through a synthesis of generative-era experimental evidence, including two original meta-analytic pools, combined with a conceptual task-delegation taxonomy. Across five controlled experiments per outcome, copilot-style assistance improved output quality (pooled standardized mean difference [SMD] +0.35, 95% CI [0.24, 0.47], k = 5) and reduced completion time (pooled SMD-0.66, 95% CI [-0.80,-0.53], k = 5). Boundary evidence qualifies these averages: experienced practitioners can slow down under assistance, gains reverse on tasks beyond the current capability frontier, and individual improvements can homogenize collective output. Integrating this evidence with the levels-of-automation tradition, the article proposes a four-mode taxonomy spanning human-only work, copilot augmentation, AI teammates, and autonomous agent delegation, together with six decision dimensions, covering frontier position, output verifiability, worker expertise, stakes and reversibility, process observability, and diversity externalities, and governance criteria that scale with autonomy. The framework positions delegation as a task-level, evidencescaled, and reversible decision rather than a one-time organizational commitment to a single mode of automation.
