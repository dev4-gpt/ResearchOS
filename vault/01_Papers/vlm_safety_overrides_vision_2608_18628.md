Gupta and Chakraborty compare default instructions with instructions to answer only when information is visible. They report abstentions despite recoverable visual evidence, refusal-related decoding changes, and interventions that restore answering. Full text inspected: abstract, introduction, example instruction contrast, and research questions.

Proposal implication (our interpretation): observable refusal and perception must be measured separately. This study's conservative grounding instruction and answer abstention are not identical to refusing harmful requests, so it supplies a methodological caution rather than direct evidence of a harmful-text versus harmful-image safety seesaw.
