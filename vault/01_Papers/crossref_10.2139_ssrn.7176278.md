# Enterprise Trust Gaps in Generative AI Why Accuracy is not Enough: Evidence from Mid-market Generative AI Adoption

**Authors**: Ibe Imo, Kofi Agyare-Kwabi, Abolaji Adesoji, David Odukoya, Isaac Sarfo
**Published**: 2026-8-5 | **Citations**: 0 | **Source**: Crossref
**URL**: https://doi.org/10.2139/ssrn.7176278

## Executive Summary & Abstract
&lt;div&gt;
 While enterprise generative AI has seen rapid, widespread adoption, it faces a significant and growing trust lag. 88% of organizations have deployed AI in at least one business function, though only 39% attribute any EBIT impact to AI at the enterprise level (McKinsey, 2025). According to Deloitte's 2026 State of AI in the Enterprise, a survey of 3,235 enterprise leaders across 24 countries, only 21% of enterprises have mature governance models (Deloitte, 2026). Workers are not rejecting AI because it fails. They are rejecting it because they cannot afford to be wrong-and machines cannot be held responsible when they are.&amp;nbsp;
&lt;/div&gt;
&lt;div&gt;
 &amp;nbsp; &amp;nbsp; &amp;nbsp;We hypothesize that the Enterprise Trust Gap (the disconnect between rapid corporate AI adoption and organizational confidence) is not produced by AI inaccuracy but by three organizational conditions that precede accuracy entirely: Accountability Mismatch, The Human Correction Burden, and The Confidence Framing Effect. Wherever these conditions are as a coexistent risk, trust erodes regardless of output quality. When these conditions are addressed through organizational design, trust recovers without requiring improvements to the underlying model, its infrastructure, or the surrounding AI system.&amp;nbsp;
&lt;/div&gt;
&lt;div&gt;
 &amp;nbsp; &amp;nbsp; &amp;nbsp;This paper investigates the Enterprise Trust Gap-the phenomenon where business users abandon, edit, or override generative AI outputs even when those outputs are accurate. Drawing on empirical data from enterprise professionals across financial services, cybersecurity, and technology sectors, and secondary data analysis across three large-scale enterprise AI adoption surveys, we identify three mechanisms that produce the gap.
&lt;/div&gt;
&lt;div&gt;
 &amp;nbsp; &amp;nbsp; &amp;nbsp;First — The Accountability Mismatch. AI reliance declines when users own the
 &lt;br&gt;
 responsibility and accountability of outputs and cannot independently verify the machine’s work.
 &lt;br&gt;
 Second - The Human Correction Burden. As enterprise AI usage increases, user trust does not
 &lt;br&gt;
 necessarily follow; instead, users face a steep rise in the responsibility to independently verify,
 &lt;br&gt;
 edit, and reconstruct outputs before acting on them. Third — The Confidence Framing Effect.
 &lt;br&gt;
 Non-expert audiences read hedging as incompetence rather than calibration. Uncertainty
 &lt;br&gt;
 disclosure on output accuracy increases rejection rates.
 &lt;br&gt;
 &amp;nbsp; &amp;nbsp; &amp;nbsp;The Enterprise Trust Gap is not only a technology problem. It is an organizational design
 &lt;br&gt;
 problem rooted in the structural mismatch between how AI systems communicate confidence
 &lt;br&gt;
 and how enterprises demand defensibility.
&lt;/div&gt;
