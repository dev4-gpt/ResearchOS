# [[Evidence of Impact and Interpretational Limits of Generative AI in STEM education - A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis on Cognitive Learning Outcomes]]

## Research Questions & Claims

This study presents a systematic review and meta-analysis examining the impact of [[Generative Artificial Intelligence]] (GAI) on [[Cognitive Learning Outcomes]] in [[STEM Education]].

*   **RQ1**: To meta-analyze externally assessed cognitive outcomes when learners interact with GAI in STEM education.
*   **RQ2**: To narratively synthesize reported learner challenges when interacting with GAI in STEM education.
*   **RQ3**: To narratively synthesize supportive instructional interventions when learners interact with GAI in STEM education.

**Key Claims:**
*   Initial analysis suggests an overall positive effect of GAI in STEM education, but this is largely attributed to [[Publication Bias]].
*   Significant heterogeneity exists in the effects of GAI, which is partly explained by [[Learning Outcomes]] (knowledge vs. skills) and the [[ISAR-level]] of GAI integration.
*   GAI appears promising in STEM for targeting knowledge gains and when used to **augment** student activities rather than **substituting** them.

## Methodologies, Algorithms, & System Architecture

### Study Design
*   **Type**: Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis.
*   **Protocol Preregistration**: AsPredicted (ID: 176450, URL: https://aspredicted.org/hpbd-dk75.pdf).

### Search Strategy & Inclusion Criteria
*   **Databases**: ERIC, PsycINFO, Web of Science.
*   **Search Update**: Last updated May 7, 2026.
*   **Additional Search**: Citation tracking.
*   **Inclusion Criteria for Primary Studies**:
    *   Peer-reviewed.
    *   Quantitative studies.
    *   Published after 2017.
    *   Included a comparison/control group.
    *   Examined cognitive learning in STEM.
    *   Involved learner-GAI interaction.
*   **Screening and Coding**: Conducted by two pairs of raters independently.

### Meta-Analytic Approaches
1.  **Random-Effects Meta-Analysis**:
    *   Used for initial estimation of overall effect size and assessment of [[Heterogeneity]].
    *   Calculates Hedge's *g* for effect sizes.
2.  **Robust Bayesian Meta-Analysis (RoBMA)**:
    *   Employed to account for potential [[Publication Bias]].
    *   Provides more robust estimates of the mean effect (µ) and heterogeneity (τ).
3.  **Moderator Analysis**:
    *   Conducted to explain observed heterogeneity.
    *   **Moderators**:
        *   **Learning Outcome**: Categorized as [[Knowledge]] vs. [[Skills]].
        *   **ISAR-level (Inversion-Substitution-Augmentation-Redefinition-level)**: Compares the cognitive activity of the intervention and control groups, conceptualized in terms of the [[ICAP-level (Interactive-Constructive-Active-Passive-level)]].

## Experimental Results, Datasets, & Quantitative Benchmarks

### Search Yield & Included Studies
*   **Eligible Studies from Search**: 85.
*   **Studies Meeting Meta-Analytic Criteria**: 49 studies, yielding 59 distinct effect sizes.

### Random-Effects Meta-Analysis (RQ1)
*   **Overall Effect**: Positive effects of GAI in STEM education (specific *g* value not provided for overall effect, but context implies a positive trend before bias correction).
*   **Heterogeneity**: Substantial heterogeneity observed.
    *   `I^2 = 96.32%`
*   **Prediction Interval**: Ranges from Hedge’s `g = -1.52` to `g = 3.20`.
*   **Publication Bias Indication**: Funnel plot asymmetry suggested a potential publication bias.

### Robust Bayesian Meta-Analysis (RoBMA) Results (RQ1, Publication Bias Corrected)
*   **Overall Effect (Bias-Corrected)**: The overall positive effect of GAI can be largely attributed to publication bias.
    *   Mean effect: `µ = 0.076 ± 0.254` (mean ± standard deviation). This indicates a very small, non-significant positive effect after bias correction.
*   **Remaining Heterogeneity (Bias-Corrected)**: A large heterogeneity persists, which appears *not* to be associated with publication bias.
    *   Heterogeneity parameter: `τ = 1.190 ± 0.166` (mean ± standard deviation).

### Moderator Analysis Results
*   **Explained Heterogeneity (R²) for Moderators**:
    *   [[Learning Outcome]] (knowledge vs. skills) explained `R^2 = 12.5%` of the observed heterogeneity.
    *   [[ISAR-level]] explained `R^2 = 13.0%` of the observed heterogeneity.
*   **Robustness**: Moderator findings were robust under publication-bias correction using RoBMA.
*   **Insights from Moderators**:
    *   GAI appears promising in STEM if [[Knowledge]] gains are targeted as learning outcomes.
    *   GAI is beneficial when used to [[Augment]] students’ learning activities.
    *   GAI can be detrimental when used to [[Substitute]] students’ learning activities.

### Qualitative Findings (RQ2-RQ3)
*   Evidence for [[Learner Challenges]] and [[Supportive Instructional Interventions]] was limited and inconsistently reported.
*   Findings are presented as transparent, caveated qualitative insights rather than generalizable effect estimates.

### Specific Findings on Study Quality
*   `N = 33` studies reported large effects because the cognitive activities of the students in the intervention and control groups were not comparable.

## Limitations Acknowledged by Authors

*   The initial random-effects meta-analysis showed **substantial heterogeneity** (`I^2 = 96.32%`), indicating significant variability in effects across studies.
*   **Funnel plot asymmetry** suggested a **potential publication bias**, which was confirmed by the RoBMA.
*   The **overall positive effect of GAI** identified in initial analyses was **largely attributed to publication bias** when corrected using RoBMA, indicating a very small, non-significant true effect.
*   Evidence for [[Learner Challenges]] (RQ2) and [[Supportive Instructional Interventions]] (RQ3) was **limited and inconsistently reported**, preventing quantitative pooling and generalizable effect estimates.
*   A significant number of studies (`N = 33`) reported seemingly large effects, but these were found to be due to **incomparable cognitive activities** between the intervention and control groups, highlighting methodological limitations in primary research.
*   Despite moderator analyses explaining parts of the heterogeneity, a **large portion of the heterogeneity still remains unexplained** (`τ = 1.190 ± 0.166`).
