This document summarizes the systematic review and meta-analysis titled "Evidence of Impact and Interpretational Limits of Generative AI in STEM education - A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis on Cognitive Learning Outcomes". The study investigates the impact of [[Generative AI (GAI)]] on [[Cognitive Learning Outcomes]] in [[STEM Education]].

## [[Research Questions]]

*   **[[RQ1]]:** To what extent does [[Generative AI (GAI)]] impact externally assessed [[Cognitive Learning Outcomes]] in [[STEM Education]]? (Meta-analyzed quantitatively)
*   **[[RQ2]]:** What are the reported learner challenges when interacting with [[Generative AI (GAI)]] in [[STEM Education]]? (Narratively synthesized)
*   **[[RQ3]]:** What are the supportive instructional interventions when interacting with [[Generative AI (GAI)]] in [[STEM Education]]? (Narratively synthesized)

## [[Methodology]]

The review protocol was preregistered on [[AsPredicted]] (ID: 176450).

### Study Selection and Screening
Two pairs of independent raters screened and coded peer-reviewed quantitative studies.
**Inclusion Criteria**:
*   Published after 2017.
*   Included a comparison/control group.
*   Examined cognitive learning in STEM.
*   Involved learner-GAI interaction.

### Search Strategy
A systematic search was conducted across [[ERIC]], [[PsycINFO]], and [[Web of Science]], updated on May 7, 2026. This was supplemented by citation tracking.

### Data Extraction
From 85 eligible studies, 49 studies contributing 59 effect sizes met the criteria for [[Meta-Analysis]].

### Statistical Analysis
1.  **[[Random-Effects Meta-Analysis]]**:
    *   Used to assess the overall effect of [[Generative AI (GAI)]] on [[Cognitive Learning Outcomes]] (RQ1).
    *   Quantified heterogeneity using [[I2 statistic]].
    *   Calculated a [[Prediction Interval]].
    *   Assessed [[Publication Bias]] through [[Funnel Plot Asymmetry]].

2.  **[[Robust Bayesian Meta-Analysis (RoBMA)]]**:
    *   Conducted to account for potential [[Publication Bias]].
    *   Provided a robust estimate of the overall effect (µ) and heterogeneity (τ).

3.  **[[Moderator Analysis]]**:
    *   Conducted to explain observed heterogeneity.
    *   Moderator variables:
        *   [[Learning Outcome Type]]: Categorized as "knowledge" vs. "skills".
        *   [[ISAR-Level (Inversion-Substitution-Augmentation-Redefinition Level)]]: Compares the cognitive activity of intervention and control groups based on the [[ICAP-Level (Interactive-Constructive-Active-Passive Level)]] framework.

4.  **Narrative Synthesis**:
    *   For [[RQ2]] and [[RQ3]], qualitative findings regarding learner challenges and supportive interventions were narratively synthesized due to limited and inconsistently reported evidence, making quantitative pooling not feasible.

## [[Key Findings]]

### Overall Effect (RQ1)
*   **[[Random-Effects Meta-Analysis]]**:
    *   Showed an overall positive effect of [[Generative AI (GAI)]] in [[STEM Education]].
    *   **Substantial Heterogeneity**: I2 = 96.32%.
    *   **Prediction Interval**: Hedge’s g = -1.52 to g = 3.20.
    *   [[Funnel Plot Asymmetry]] suggested a potential [[Publication Bias]].

*   **[[Robust Bayesian Meta-Analysis (RoBMA)]]**:
    *   The overall positive effect of [[Generative AI (GAI)]] can be largely attributed to [[Publication Bias]].
    *   **Overall Effect (µ)**: 0.076 ± 0.254 (indicating a very small, non-significant effect when accounting for bias).
    *   **Large Heterogeneity (τ)**: 1.190 ± 0.166, which appeared not to be associated with [[Publication Bias]].

### Moderator Analysis
*   Two moderators explained parts of the observed heterogeneity:
    *   [[Learning Outcome Type]] (knowledge vs. skills): R2 = 12.5%.
    *   [[ISAR-Level (Inversion-Substitution-Augmentation-Redefinition Level)]]: R2 = 13.0%.
*   The moderator findings were robust under [[Publication Bias]] correction using [[RoBMA]], unlike the main effect.

### Interpretational Limits and Specific Insights
*   **Impact of Learning Outcome Type**: [[RoBMA]] indicates that [[Generative AI (GAI)]] appears promising in STEM if [[Knowledge Gains]] are targeted as [[Learning Outcomes]].
*   **Impact of ISAR-Level**: [[Generative AI (GAI)]] is beneficial when used to *augment* students’ learning activities, but can be detrimental if it *substitutes* their activities.
*   **Non-Comparable Cognitive Activities**: Numerous studies (N = 33) reported large effects primarily because the cognitive activities of students in the intervention and control groups were not comparable. This highlights a significant confound in many primary studies.

### Learner Challenges and Interventions (RQ2 & RQ3)
*   Evidence for [[RQ2]] and [[RQ3]] was limited and inconsistently reported.
*   Findings are presented as transparent, caveated qualitative insights rather than generalizable effect estimates.

## [[Quantitative Benchmarks]] & [[Mathematical Equations]]

*   **Number of eligible studies**: N = 85
*   **Number of studies meeting meta-analytic criteria**: N = 49
*   **Number of effect sizes**: N = 59
*   **Overall effect (Random-Effects)**: Positive effect (specific Hedge's g not given for overall effect, only prediction interval)
*   **Heterogeneity (Random-Effects)**: $I^2 = 96.32\%$
*   **Prediction Interval (Hedge's g)**: $[-1.52, 3.20]$
*   **RoBMA Overall Effect (µ)**: $\mu = 0.076 \pm 0.254$
*   **RoBMA Heterogeneity (τ)**: $\tau = 1.190 \pm 0.166$
*   **Moderator Analysis - Explained Heterogeneity ($R^2$)**:
    *   [[Learning Outcome Type]]: $R^2 = 12.5\%$
    *   [[ISAR-Level (Inversion-Substitution-Augmentation-Redefinition Level)]]: $R^2 = 13.0\%$

## [[Limitations]]

*   **[[Publication Bias]]**: The initial [[Random-Effects Meta-Analysis]] showed [[Funnel Plot Asymmetry]], suggesting potential [[Publication Bias]]. [[RoBMA]] confirmed that the overall positive effect was largely attributable to this bias.
*   **Substantial Heterogeneity**: A very high [[I2 statistic]] ($96.32\%$) and a large [[τ]] (1.190) indicate significant variability between study effects, which was only partially explained by the chosen moderators ($R^2 \approx 12-13\%$).
*   **Non-Comparable Cognitive Activities**: A notable number of studies (N=33) reported large effects due to a lack of comparability in cognitive activities between intervention and control groups, limiting the interpretability of primary study findings.
*   **Limited Evidence for RQ2-RQ3**: Data on learner challenges and supportive interventions were scarce and inconsistently reported, preventing quantitative synthesis and yielding only qualitative, non-generalizable insights.
*   **Interpreting RoBMA results**: While [[RoBMA]] helps correct for [[Publication Bias]], the remaining large heterogeneity (τ = 1.190) suggests that other unmeasured factors contribute significantly to the variability in GAI effects.
