## Abstract
We study the staggered introduction of a generative AI-based conversational assistant using data from 5,179 customer support agents. Access to the tool increases productivity, as measured by issues resolved per hour, by 14 percent on average, with the greatest impact on novice and low-skilled workers, and minimal impact on experienced and highly skilled workers. We provide suggestive evidence that the AI model disseminates the potentially tacit knowledge of more able workers and helps newer workers move down the experience curve. In addition, we show that AI assistance improves customer sentiment, reduces requests for managerial intervention, and improves employee retention.

## 1. Research Framework & Core Hypotheses

The paper examines the real-world economic and productivity impacts of integrating a **generative AI-based conversational assistant** into a highly specialized, real-world workplace setting.

### Key Hypotheses
1. **Productivity Enhancement**: Access to generative AI tools increases worker output, specifically measured via operational throughput metrics.
2. **Heterogeneous Skill Impact (Un-skilling/Equalizing Effect)**: Generative AI disproportionately benefits novice and low-skilled workers compared to highly experienced or high-skilled workers, acting as an equalizer.
3. **Knowledge Dissemination**: The generative AI model captures and formalizes the tacit knowledge of top-performing agents, effectively transferring these skills to newer or lower-performing workers.
4. **Intangible Operational Benefits**: AI assistance improves non-throughput dimensions of job quality, such as [[Customer Sentiment]], managerial escalations, and [[Employee Retention]].

## 2. Methodology & Study Design

The authors exploit a natural experiment created by the **staggered rollout** of an AI conversational assistant within a large organization.

### Study Demographics & Sample Size
- **Subject Pool**: $N = 5,179$ customer support agents.
- **Intervention**: Access to a generative AI conversational assistant providing real-time response recommendations and contextual knowledge prompts.

### Econometric Specification
To evaluate the causal impact of the generative AI tool, the study leverages a **Staggered Difference-in-Differences (Event Study)** design. The implicit causal model can be formalized as:

$$Y_{it} = \alpha_i + \tau_t + \beta \text{AI\_Access}_{it} + \epsilon_{it}$$

Where:
- $Y_{it}$ represents the productivity metric (e.g., log of resolved issues per hour) for agent $i$ at time $t$.
- $\alpha_i$ represents **Agent Fixed Effects**, controlling for time-invariant individual ability.
- $\tau_t$ represents **Time Fixed Effects** (e.g., day, week, or month of observation), controlling for macro-level shocks and seasonal trends.
- $\text{AI\_Access}_{it}$ is an indicator variable equal to $1$ if agent $i$ has active access to the generative AI tool at time $t$, and $0$ otherwise.
- $\beta$ is the parameter of interest, capturing the average treatment effect of generative AI access on agent productivity.

## 3. Quantitative Results & Benchmarks

The staggered rollout yielded clear, statistically significant impacts across multiple performance dimensions:

### Productivity Metrics
| Outcome Metric | Observed Impact | Statistical/Practical Significance |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Average Productivity** (Issues resolved per hour) | **+14%** increase on average | Highly significant; driven by faster resolution speeds and multi-tasking capacity. |
| **Novice & Low-Skilled Workers** | **Maximum positive impact** | Significant acceleration along the experience curve. |
| **Experienced & High-Skilled Workers** | **Minimal to zero impact** | The tool did not significantly alter the throughput of top-tier performers. |

- **Customer Sentiment**: Measurable improvement in post-interaction customer feedback ratings.

- **Employee Retention**: Improved job satisfaction translating directly into reduced turnover and higher employee retention rates.

## 4. Mechanisms: Tacit Knowledge & Learning Curves

The authors provide suggestive evidence for how these productivity gains are realized:

1. **Tacit Knowledge Extraction**: Generative AI models are trained on historical interaction data, effectively encoding the implicit strategies and verbal patterns of highly skilled workers.
2. **Knowledge Redistribution**: When deployed, the AI tool serves as a real-time transmission mechanism, disseminating these best practices to novice agents who have not yet developed this internal tacit knowledge.
3. **Experience Curve Acceleration**: Newer workers using the AI tool move down the experience curve (learning rate) significantly faster than their historical control group counterparts.

---

## 5. Limitations & Stated Assumptions

While the study presents robust empirical evidence of AI-induced productivity gains, several limitations are noted or inherent in the design:

- **Industry Specificity**: The dataset is restricted to customer support agents ($N = 5,179$). Generalizability to more open-ended, non-scripted, or highly physical tasks remains an open question.
- **Suggestive Evidence on Tacit Knowledge**: The mechanism of "tacit knowledge dissemination" is supported by suggestive evidence rather than direct, causal tracing of specific linguistic semantic shifts from experts to novice AI outputs.
- **Long-term Skill Degradation**: The study does not fully observe whether relying on real-time AI suggestions over long horizons inhibits the organic development of novice agents' independent problem-solving skills (potential "deskilling" over multi-year horizons).
