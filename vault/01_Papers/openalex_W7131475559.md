## [[Abstract]] Summary

This paper presents a Systematization of Knowledge (SoK) regarding [[Agentic Skills]], which are reusable procedural capabilities for LLM agents to execute long-horizon workflows reliably. The authors define skills as callable modules packaging procedural knowledge with explicit applicability conditions, execution policies, termination criteria, and reusable interfaces. The paper maps the skill layer across its full lifecycle (discovery, practice, distillation, storage, composition, evaluation, and update) and introduces two complementary taxonomies:
1.  A system-level taxonomy of **seven design patterns** for skill packaging and execution.
2.  An orthogonal **representation $\times$ scope** taxonomy detailing what skills are (natural language, code, policy, hybrid) and their operating environments (web, OS, software engineering, robotics).

The paper further analyzes [[Security and Governance Implications]] for skill-based agents, including [[supply-chain risks]], [[prompt injection via skill payloads]], and [[trust-tiered execution]], substantiated by a [[Case Study: ClawHavoc Campaign]] involving 1,200 malicious skills. It surveys [[Evaluation Approaches]], noting that curated skills improve agent success rates while self-generated skills may degrade them. The work concludes by outlining open challenges for robust, verifiable, and certifiable real-world autonomous agent skills.

## [[Key Concepts]]

*   **[[Agentic Skills]]**: Reusable procedural capabilities used by agentic systems to execute long-horizon workflows reliably. They are callable modules that package procedural knowledge with explicit applicability conditions, execution policies, termination criteria, and reusable interfaces. Unlike one-off plans or atomic tool calls, skills are designed to operate across multiple tasks effectively.

## [[Security and Governance Implications]]

The paper analyzes critical security and governance issues for skill-based agents:
*   [[Supply-chain risks]]: Risks associated with the origin and dependencies of skills.
*   [[Prompt injection via skill payloads]]: Malicious skills injecting prompts to compromise agents.
*   [[Trust-tiered execution]]: Mechanisms for executing skills based on their trustworthiness level.

### [[Case Study: ClawHavoc Campaign]]

*   **Description**: A real-world campaign where nearly 1,200 malicious skills infiltrated a major agent marketplace.
*   **Impact**: These malicious skills were designed to exfiltrate sensitive data, including API keys, cryptocurrency wallets, and browser credentials, at scale.
*   **Quantitative Data**: N = 1,200 malicious skills.

## [[Evaluation Approaches]]

The paper surveys various [[deterministic evaluation]] approaches for agentic skills.

### Quantitative Benchmarks

*   **Curated Skills**: Recent benchmark evidence suggests that curated skills can *substantially improve agent success rates*.
*   **Self-Generated Skills**: Recent benchmark evidence suggests that self-generated skills *may degrade agent success rates*.

## [[Stated Limitations and Open Challenges]]

The paper concludes by highlighting open challenges for the field, which serve as acknowledged limitations of current skill-based agent systems:
*   Achieving **robust skills** for real-world autonomous agents.
*   Developing **verifiable skills** to ensure correctness and safety.
*   Establishing **certifiable skills** for regulatory compliance and trustworthiness.
