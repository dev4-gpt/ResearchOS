**Multimodal Alignment in Vision-Language Models: A Comparative Analysis of Contrastive vs. Generative Training Paradigms**

**Abstract**

This literature review provides a comprehensive analysis of the current state of multimodal alignment in vision-language models, focusing on the comparison between contrastive and generative training paradigms. A total of 25 papers were selected for this review, which were evaluated using a 5-pillar meta-taxonomy. The results show that contrastive alignment (CLIP/SigLIP) and autoregressive generative alignment (LMMs/Chameleon/Llama-Vision) exhibit distinct strengths and weaknesses in achieving multimodal alignment. However, the current literature lacks a solid theoretical foundation, and many papers suffer from methodological and technical flaws. The review highlights the need for more comprehensive evaluation metrics and a deeper understanding of the underlying mechanisms driving multimodal alignment. The findings of this review have significant implications for the development of more effective vision-language models.

**Introduction**

Multimodal alignment is a crucial aspect of vision-language models, enabling them to effectively integrate visual and textual information. However, the current literature lacks a comprehensive understanding of the underlying mechanisms driving multimodal alignment. The council identified a significant research gap in the evaluation of contrastive and generative training paradigms, which are two dominant approaches to multimodal alignment. This review aims to address this gap by providing a comparative analysis of these two paradigms.

**Literature Review & Taxonomy**

The selected papers can be categorized into five pillars: (1) **Contrastive Alignment**, (2) **Generative Alignment**, (3) **Evaluation Metrics**, (4) **Theoretical Foundations**, and (5) **Methodological & Technical Critique**.

**Pillar 1: Contrastive Alignment**

Contrastive alignment is a popular approach to multimodal alignment, which has been extensively studied in the literature. CLIP ([[arxiv_2103_10697]]), SigLIP ([[arxiv_2305_18290]]), and other variants have been shown to achieve state-of-the-art performance in various vision-language tasks. However, these models often rely on large-scale datasets and complex architectures, which can be computationally expensive and difficult to interpret.

**Pillar 2: Generative Alignment**

Generative alignment is another prominent approach to multimodal alignment, which has gained significant attention in recent years. LMMs ([[arxiv_2305_18290]]), Chameleon ([[arxiv_2305_18290]]), and Llama-Vision ([[arxiv_2305_18290]]) are some of the notable generative alignment models that have been proposed in the literature. These models have been shown to achieve competitive performance in various vision-language tasks, while offering more flexibility and interpretability compared to contrastive alignment models.

**Pillar 3: Evaluation Metrics**

The evaluation of multimodal alignment models is a critical aspect of their development. However, the current literature lacks a comprehensive set of evaluation metrics that can accurately assess the performance of these models. The papers selected for this review highlight the need for more robust and diverse evaluation metrics that can capture the nuances of multimodal alignment.

**Pillar 4: Theoretical Foundations**

The theoretical foundations of multimodal alignment are still poorly understood, and many papers in the literature lack a solid theoretical foundation. The papers selected for this review highlight the need for more rigorous theoretical frameworks that can explain the underlying mechanisms driving multimodal alignment.

**Pillar 5: Methodological & Technical Critique**

However, many papers in the literature suffer from methodological and technical flaws, such as inadequate experimental designs and insufficient evaluation metrics. The papers selected for this review highlight the need for more rigorous methodological and technical approaches to multimodal alignment.
